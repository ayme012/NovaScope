    Click any star on the map, or pick one from this list, to see where it is, fun facts about it, and how it changes by the year on the dial.

      Each journey turns the sky to the right spot and runs the clock for you.

      How this works

      Each star moves in a straight line through space using its measured sideways motion, its speed toward or away from us, and its distance. Its brightness changes with distance. Earth's slow wobble (precession, one turn every 25,772 years) moves the celestial poles.

      Limits: only stars brighter than magnitude 7 today are shown, so a faint star that will move close later is missing. Over hundreds of thousands of years real paths curve as stars orbit the galaxy.

      IRSA's cutout tutorial finds every band 2 image of M101 and keeps only a small square around the galaxy from each one, so it downloads a few kilobytes instead of whole images.

      TargetM101, RA 210.80227°, Dec +54.34895° Cutout size0.1° (6 arcminutes) Band2 (SPHEREx-D2, 1.10–1.62 µm) DataDR1 and QR3 Images found84 Search timeabout 20 s Download2.3 min one by one, about 13 s with 10 at once Outputspherex_cutouts_mef.fits
      1. Search IRSA's SPHEREx tables for band 2 images that contain M101, oldest first.
      2. Add ?center=RA,Dec&size=0.1 to each image link, so IRSA sends only the cutout.
      3. Download the cutouts ten at a time.
      4. Pack them into one FITS file with a CUTOUT_INFO table of dates and wavelengths.

      The viewer at the top of this tab opens that file. Drop spherex_cutouts_mef.fits in the box and press Play to watch every cutout in date order.
